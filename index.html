Why this order

Most people who want to buy a small business start with the spreadsheet. That’s the third question.

A searcher’s scarcest resource is rarely capital — it’s attention over a long, unglamorous search. Stanford’s search fund studies put median time to acquisition at roughly 20 months, and a substantial minority of searches never close at all. Every hour spent modeling a business you were never going to buy, or were never suited to run, is taken from the search that would have found the one you were.

So: disqualify yourself first (it’s free), disqualify the business second (it’s cheap), and only then argue about price (it’s expensive, and it’s the only one that needs a lawyer).

An educational model, not advice. It will not replace a quality-of-earnings report, a lender’s credit memo, or a transaction attorney. Use it to decide what deserves those things.

Where the numbers come from

SBA 7(a) terms

Maximum loan $5,000,000. Business-acquisition and goodwill loans amortize over a maximum of 10 years, with no SBA prepayment penalty at that maturity. Variable rates cap at Prime + 3.00% for loans over $350,000 — with the Wall Street Journal Prime Rate at 6.75% (its level since December 2025; this page does not update, so check it) that is a 9.75% ceiling, and acquisition paper typically prices at Prime + 2.50–3.00%. Minimum equity injection for a change of ownership is 10% of total project cost; a seller note can count for at most half of that, and only if it sits on full standby — no principal and no interest paid — for the life of the SBA loan.

Amortization

Debt service runs on a true monthly schedule, rolled up annually, so each year’s interest/principal split is exact. Approximating annual interest as opening balance × rate overstates it by roughly half a year of amortization; this model doesn’t do that.

Taxes

Taxed on EBITDA − D&A − interest, floored at zero. Interest is deductible; principal is not, which is exactly why a highly levered small business can look profitable and still be cash-poor. Most SBA acquisitions are asset purchases, which step up basis and amortize goodwill over 15 years under IRC §197 — a real shield this model approximates through the D&A input.

The IRR solver

Bracketed bisection over a 2,000-point scan of [−100%, +1000%], not Newton’s method. It finds every root the scan separates; roots closer together than about half a percentage point, and returns above +1000%, fall outside it, and the model says so rather than guessing. Where several roots exist it flags them — a spreadsheet’s IRR() silently returns whichever sat nearest its guess. The classic two-root case [−100, 230, −132] correctly returns both 10% and 20%.

Base rates worth remembering

Stanford’s search fund studies report a strong aggregate return for the asset class, but the distribution is brutal: a substantial share of completed acquisitions lose money outright, and many searches never close at all. Any model that assumes nothing goes wrong — including this one, unless you make it — is not a model of the asset class. Run a downside case where growth is negative and the exit multiple compresses.

On the frameworks

“Double Diamond” names two different things. In design it is the UK Design Council’s model of the innovation process — Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver — where each diamond diverges then converges. In acquisition entrepreneurship, practitioners use the same diverge/converge shape as a scorecard for rating targets.

The scorecard here is the acquisition reading, built from criteria standard across buy-side screening: customer fragmentation, revenue recurrence, management depth, capex intensity, owner dependence, financeability, quality of earnings, transition risk. It is an independent implementation of a common approach, not a reproduction of any particular practitioner’s proprietary scorecard — if you are working from a specific published framework, use its weights rather than these.

The ownership ladder in Gate I follows the same logic: the distinction between an employee, an equity employee, an owner, and an owner-operator is a common framing in the literature, and the four capacities scored here were chosen because they are the ones an SBA personal guarantee puts to the test.

Limits — read before you rely on this
  • No search costs, no owner salary during the hold, no transaction costs at exit.
  • No tax on the gain at sale — exit proceeds arrive untaxed, which flatters IRR by several points.
  • Fixed interest rate. Real SBA variable loans reset quarterly against Prime.
  • No working-capital peg true-up, no earnout, no real estate, no revolver.
  • No probability of impairment. The model cannot lose you money unless you tell it to.
  • Flat tax rate. Entity choice, state tax, and pass-through treatment all matter and none are here.
  • SBA rules change. The 50 10 series Standard Operating Procedure is revised regularly and the equity-injection and standby provisions move with it. Confirm current terms with a lender.
Not investment, legal, or tax advice. This is a teaching model. Before you sign anything, get a quality-of-earnings report, a transaction attorney, and a lender’s term sheet.
